### Fixed

- An average, median or anomaly bound under a scaled format keeps the decimals it needs. On a column, bar, line or combo chart with format `1M`, an average of 1.25M printed as `1M`, because its decimals were counted on 1,250,000 and not on 1.25. The label now counts them at the printed scale and shows `1.3M`, and `1.25M` under a format with two decimals.
- A mean `valueLine` prints the true average on every chart that draws one. On a line, column or dot chart, an average of 1.25M under format `1M` printed `1M` and under `1.0M` printed `1.3M`, and an average of 2,500 under `$1k` printed `$3k`, while a bar printed `1.3M`, `1.25M` and `$2.5k`. Under a format, all charts now print the bar's text, and a dot chart's `averageMarker` does too. Without a format, a bar and a dot print `Average 8.06` where a column and a line print `Average 8.1`. A mean under an authored format that rounds it, such as `0.00` on a variance chart, shows one decimal more (`5.333` for 5.3333), as on a bar. With no format, a column average of 1.25M prints `1.25M` where it printed `1.3M`, and anomaly bounds print `800.818k` and `1.579M` where they printed `800.82k` and `1.58M`. Under format `0%` or `0`, a mean `valueLine` of 23.5 on a column, line or dot prints `23.5%` or `23.5` where it printed `24%` or `24`, as on a bar. A longer average label can hide a neighbouring data label: under `1.0M`, a combo average of 1.57M printed `1.6M` and now prints `1.57M`, and a label it overlaps moves to a note with a warning. An authored `format` on the line itself still wins, and gives back the old text.
