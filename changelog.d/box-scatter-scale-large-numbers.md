### Changed / may need action

- A box chart and a scatter chart without a `format` scale numbers from a million, as a bar already does, when they render for a target (`report`, `slide` or `dashboard`). The scale comes once from the chart's largest value, and each scatter axis takes its own. A box chart prints `2.78B` and `59.70M` where it printed `2,778,700,000` and `59,700,000`, on its labels and its ticks (`0`, `2B`, `4B`). Its precision comes from the printed statistics, outliers and small-group points, not from every observation. A scatter axis prints `60M`, `500M`, `1.0B` and `2.0B` where it printed `60,000,000` and `2,000,000,000`. Its point coordinates in descriptions and hover texts print at the same scale. A log axis prints each tick at its own scale. A linear axis prints every tick at the scale of its largest tick (`0.2B` to `1.0B`, never `1,000M`). A quadrant cut prints at its own scale with every digit it needs (`452.5M`). A unit that names a scale (`EUR m`) and an authored `format` or `axisFormat` keep the digits. To get the old look back, set `format: '1,000'` on a box chart, or `axisFormat: { x: '1,000', y: '1,000' }` on a scatter chart.
- A German chart prints millions as ` Mio.` beside ` Mrd.` wherever the engine picks the scale, on every chart type: `500 Mio.` where it printed `500M`, and `$10.6 Mio.` on a KPI where it printed `$10.6M`. A German KPI card with comparisons at 720 px can stack its longer headline above the comparison rows. A table unit in a German header or subtitle keeps the space of the short form: `$ Mio.` and `$ Mrd.`, where a billion unit printed `$Mrd.`. A format example with the letter `M`, such as `format: '1,0M'`, keeps the letter.
